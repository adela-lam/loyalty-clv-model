What is each customer really worth?

Model what a loyalty program, referrals, replenishment subscriptions and a paid membership add to customer lifetime value, after rewards, discounts, perks and platform fees. Then see which assumption matters most before you sign a vendor contract.

Assumptions

Example numbers for a made-up lifestyle brand. Change any of them.

Where the extra value comes from

Lifetime value per new customer over the time horizon, today's dollars

Payback

Profit earned per customer to date. A customer pays back once the line passes what it cost to acquire them.

Program by program

Incremental value is net of rewards, discounts and perks. Platform fees are shown separately.

What matters most

Change in lifetime value if each assumption is 20% lower or higher. Pressure-test the top few before you commit.

How it works

Everything is modelled for one new customer, month by month, as contribution (revenue × margin, after program costs), discounted back to today. Month 0 is the first order.

baseline = first order + Σ (chance still active × repeat orders × order value × margin × discount factor)
loyalty = join rate × (member value − baseline value), members buy more often, stay longer and earn rewards
referral = expected referrals × (referred customer's value × (1 + premium) − rewards paid)
replenishment = adoption × Σ subscription orders × [non-cannibalised share × (margin − discount) − cannibalised share × discount]
membership = adoption × (yearly fee − perk cost while members renew + extra orders from members)
lifetime value = baseline + programs − platform fees ÷ new customers per month

Assumptions to know

  • Programs are treated as independent, so their gains add up. In reality they overlap, so treat the total as an upper bound and the per-program numbers as the guide.
  • Referred customers are worth the baseline value plus a premium. Their own referrals aren't counted, which keeps the estimate conservative.
  • Replenishment counts only extra orders. The cannibalisation setting removes orders the customer would have placed anyway at full price.
  • Platform fees are fixed monthly costs, spread across the customers you acquire each month.

Research behind the defaults

  • Schmitt, Skiera & Van den Bulte, "Referral Programs and Customer Value", Journal of Marketing (2011): referred customers were about 16% more valuable, which sets the default referral premium.
  • Kumar, Petersen & Leone, "How Valuable Is Word of Mouth?", Harvard Business Review (2007): a customer's referral value is separate from their purchase value, and the two should be measured separately.
  • Reichheld & Sasser, "Zero Defections", Harvard Business Review (1990): small gains in retention compound into large gains in profit.
  • Gupta, Lehmann & Stuart, "Valuing Customers", Journal of Marketing Research (2004): the retention-based CLV approach used here.
